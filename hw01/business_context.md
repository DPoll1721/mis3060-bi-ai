Question 1: Portfolio Committee Priorities

Prompt sent:
"Our portfolio has five loan purposes — Auto, Personal, Home Improvement, Education, and Business — with very different average loan sizes and risk profiles. What would a portfolio committee typically want to see in a quarterly review for a lender with this specific mix, and would the reporting priorities differ by loan purpose?"

Summary of response:
Claude explained that while a portfolio committee tracks the same core metrics across all five loan purposes — balance growth, delinquency trends, charge-offs, and yield by segment — which metric matters most shifts depending on the type of loan. Collateralized loans like Auto and Home Improvement get extra attention on loan-to-value and recovery rates since collateral value affects loss severity, while unsecured Personal loans are judged more on default frequency and credit-score drift. Education loans are evaluated differently still, with a focus on deferment status and long-term cohort performance since repayment often starts later. Business loans, despite being the smallest segment by loan count, get the most individualized scrutiny — concentration limits, covenant compliance, and loan-by-loan tracking — because of their size and risk concentration. Beyond these segment-specific views, the committee also wants portfolio-wide context like mix shift, risk-adjusted returns, and a narrative explaining what changed and why.

Follow-up question this raised for me:
Given that Business loans get individual loan-by-loan scrutiny while the other four segments are monitored in aggregate, at what point — in dollar size, loan count, or portfolio share — would a committee typically decide that another segment (like a small but growing Education loan book) also needs that same individual-level attention instead of pooled statistics?
