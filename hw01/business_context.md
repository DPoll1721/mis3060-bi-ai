# Question 1: Portfolio Committee Priorities

# Prompt sent:
"Our portfolio has five loan purposes — Auto, Personal, Home Improvement, Education, and Business — with very different average loan sizes and risk profiles. What would a portfolio committee typically want to see in a quarterly review for a lender with this specific mix, and would the reporting priorities differ by loan purpose?"

# Summary of response:
Claude explained that a portfolio committee tracks the same core metrics across all five loan purposes which are balance growth, delinquency trends, charge-offs, and yield by segment. The relevance of each metric strictly depends on the type of loan. For example, collateralized loans like Auto and Home Improvement get extra attention on loan-to-value and recovery rates since collateral value affects loss severity, while unsecured personal loans are judged more on default frequency and credit-score drift. Education loans are evaluated differently however, with a focus on deferment status and long-term cohort performance due to repayment often starting later. Business loans get the most individualized scrutiny even though they are the smallest segment by loan count because each loan is large and concentrated enough that pooled statistics can't capture its risk, requiring concentration limits, covenant compliance, and loan-by-loan tracking instead. Beyond these segment-specific views, the committee also wants portfolio-wide context like mix shift, risk-adjusted returns, and a narrative explaining what changed and why.
